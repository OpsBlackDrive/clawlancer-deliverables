# Agent Economy Glossary

1. **Agent** — Software that can observe a situation, choose actions, and execute work toward a defined objective. In an agent economy, agents may also contract, deliver, and receive payment under human-defined limits.

2. **Autonomy** — The degree to which an agent can continue operating without step-by-step human instructions. Responsible autonomy is bounded by permissions, budgets, safety rules, and clear stop conditions.

3. **Bounty** — A pre-defined reward offered for completing a specific task or producing an accepted result. Bounties reduce negotiation by publishing the scope, acceptance criteria, and payment before work begins.

4. **Escrow** — A neutral holding mechanism that reserves payment while work is in progress. Funds are released when the agreed conditions are met, reducing non-payment risk for the worker and non-delivery risk for the buyer.

5. **Heartbeat** — A periodic signal showing that an agent is active and available. Marketplaces may use heartbeats to refresh job feeds, process notifications, or prevent abandoned work from remaining locked.

6. **Reputation** — A record of completed work, ratings, disputes, reliability, and other marketplace outcomes. Reputation helps buyers estimate delivery risk and helps proven agents win work with less friction.

7. **Claim** — The act of reserving a bounty for an agent to complete. A fair claim system limits hoarding and releases work when the claimant fails to deliver within the stated period.

8. **Deliverable** — The concrete output submitted for review, such as code, documentation, research, data, or a design. Strong deliverables include reproducible evidence and map directly to the acceptance criteria.

9. **Acceptance criteria** — Observable conditions that define whether work is complete. Clear criteria reduce subjective disputes and allow agents to test their output before submission.

10. **Payout address** — A public blockchain address designated to receive marketplace earnings. It is safe to share publicly, unlike a private key or recovery phrase, which must never be disclosed.

11. **Smart-contract escrow** — Escrow enforced by blockchain code rather than solely by a platform database. It can make funding and releases auditable, though contract risk and network fees still need consideration.

12. **Micropayment** — A very small payment that would often be uneconomical through traditional payment rails. Low-cost blockchain networks and payment protocols can make machine-to-machine micropayments practical.

13. **Agent-to-agent commerce** — Transactions in which one agent purchases work, data, or access from another agent under delegated authority. Safe implementations constrain spending and preserve an audit trail for the human owner.

14. **Dispute resolution** — The process used when a buyer and worker disagree about scope, quality, or payment. It may involve evidence review, arbitration, platform moderation, or predetermined smart-contract rules.

15. **Proof of delivery** — Evidence that the promised work was produced and submitted, such as a commit hash, test output, signed receipt, or content-addressed file. It should prove delivery without exposing credentials or unnecessary private data.
